## 1. Skin Types & Barrier Function
The stratum corneum is the outermost layer of the epidermis, acting as the primary barrier against water loss and external pathogens. 
- **Oily Skin**: Characterized by hyperactive sebaceous glands producing excess sebum. Prone to enlarged pores, comedones, and shiny appearance. Benefit from non-comedogenic hydration, light weight humectants, BHA (salicylic acid), and Niacinamide.
- **Dry Skin**: Lacks natural epidermal lipids and ceramides. Characterized by tightness, flaking, fine lines, and dullness. Requires barrier-supporting lipid replenishment (ceramides, squalane, fatty acids) and occlusive moisturizers.
- **Combination Skin**: Shows oiliness in the T-zone (forehead, nose, chin) alongside normal or dry cheeks. Responds well to targeted multi-zonal routines or lightweight balancing formulations.
- **Sensitive Skin**: Heightened reactivity to topicals, temperature, or friction. Frequently exhibits erythema (redness), burning, or stinging. Requires fragrance-free, alcohol-free formulations enriched with soothing actives (Centella Asiatica, Allantoin, Colloidal Oatmeal).

## 2. Common Skincare Concerns & Contributing Factors

### Acne & Breakouts (Comedones, Papules, Pustules)
- **Contributing Factors**: Excess sebum output, follicular hyperkeratinization (clogged dead skin cells), proliferation of *Cutibacterium acnes*, and inflammatory response.
- **Evidence-Based Active Ingredients**:
  - *Salicylic Acid (BHA 0.5% - 2%)*: Soluble in oil, penetrates follicular pores to dissolve comedogenic plugs.
  - *Benzoyl Peroxide (2.5% - 5%)*: Antimicrobial oxidizing agent that reduces C. acnes without bacterial resistance.
  - *Azelaic Acid (10%)*: Anti-inflammatory, antibacterial, and keratin-regulating.
  - *Niacinamide (2% - 5%)*: Reduces sebum secretion rate and calms post-acne redness.
- **What to Avoid**: Heavy pore-clogging waxes/oils (high comedogenic rating), harsh physical scrubbing pads, over-cleansing (more than 2x daily), and squeezing lesions.

### Skin Irritation & Barrier Compromise
- **Contributing Factors**: Over-exfoliation (using AHA/BHA/Retinoids simultaneously), harsh surfactant cleansers (high pH soap bars), environmental cold/wind, allergic contact dermatitis.
- **Evidence-Based Active Ingredients**:
  - *Ceramides*: Replenish essential stratum corneum matrix.
  - *Centella Asiatica (Cica / Madecassoside)*: Accelerates epithelial repair and reduces erythema.
  - *Panthenol (Pro-Vitamin B5)*: Deeply hydrates and calms stinging.
- **What to Avoid**: Synthetic fragrances, high concentration denatured alcohol, essential oils (citrus, eucalyptus, lavender), active exfoliants or retinoids until barrier recovers fully.

### Hyperpigmentation & Dark Spots
- **Contributing Factors**: Post-inflammatory hyperpigmentation (PIH) following acne or injury, UV radiation triggering melanogenesis, hormonal melasma.
- **Evidence-Based Active Ingredients**:
  - *Azelaic Acid*: Tyrosinase inhibitor targeting hyperactive melanocytes.
  - *Niacinamide*: Prevents melanosome transfer from melanocytes to keratinocytes.
  - *Vitamin C (L-Ascorbic Acid)*: Neutralizes free radicals and inhibits melanin synthesis.
  - *Daily Broad-Spectrum Sunscreen (SPF 30+)*: Essential to prevent further darkness.

## 3. Safe Product Introduction & Patch Testing Protocol
Introducing new skincare actives requires caution to prevent contact dermatitis or barrier disruption.

### Patch Testing Protocol
1. Apply a dime-sized amount of product to a discreet test patch (such as the inner forearm or behind the ear).
2. Leave for 24-48 hours and monitor for signs of itching, redness, swelling, or burning.
3. If no reaction occurs, test on a small area of the jawline for another 2 days before full facial application.

### Introducing Strong Actives (Retinoids, AHAs, BHAs)
- **Frequency**: Start by using 1 to 2 nights per week.
- **Sandwich Technique for Sensitive Skin**: Apply moisturizer first, follow with active ingredient, and finish with a second layer of moisturizer to cushion skin.
- **Do Not Mix**: Avoid combining strong retinoids, high-strength AHAs, and Benzoyl Peroxide in the exact same evening routine step.

## 4. Medical Guardrails & When to See a Dermatologist

### Diagnostic Boundaries
The assistant provides educational information and product matching. It does **not** diagnose medical skin conditions (such as eczema, psoriasis, rosacea, cystic acne grade IV, or skin cancers). Always use non-diagnostic phrasing ("This presentation may be consistent with...").

### Red Flag Symptoms Requiring Professional Medical Evaluation
Seek prompt care from a board-certified dermatologist or medical professional if experiencing:
- Rapidly spreading redness, extreme swelling, warmth, or severe pain.
- Pustular lesions accompanied by fever, chills, or systemic illness.
- Open wounds, oozing yellow crusts, or signs of secondary bacterial/fungal infection.
- Changing, asymmetric moles with irregular borders or bleeding lesions.
- Severe recalcitrant cystic acne unresponsive to over-the-counter care.
